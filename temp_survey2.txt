2. RESEARCH GAPS 
2.1 Need for Multi-Factor Continuous Prediction
A large body of student-performance research treats the task as classification, such as pass/fail or at-risk prediction. However, your dataset provides a continuous 
Exam_Score
, making regression a natural primary task. Existing literature demonstrates both classification and regression, but classification remains common.
2.2 Need for Explainable Predictions
High prediction accuracy alone does not tell a student or administrator why a prediction was produced. Recent research increasingly uses SHAP and related XAI methods to identify the contribution of individual features and improve trust in educational models.
2.3 Limited Translation from Prediction to Action
Many studies stop after predicting performance or risk. Recent work is beginning to connect prediction with support systems, recommendations and early intervention. This creates an opportunity for our system to move from “predicting a score” toward “using the prediction to suggest areas for improvement.”
2.4 Need for Evaluation Across Multiple Models
Different student datasets behave differently, so selecting one algorithm without comparison can be misleading. Research therefore supports comparing several regression approaches rather than assuming one model is universally best.
2.5 Dataset-Specific Interpretability
Even when SHAP is used, feature importance can vary depending on the dataset and educational setting. Therefore, our system should derive explanations specifically from the Student Performance Factors dataset, rather than importing conclusions from another dataset. Recent research shows why model explanations should be examined in the context of the actual educational data.
2.6 From Analysis to an Early-Intervention System
The literature increasingly identifies the value of early identification of students needing support. Our dataset provides factors such as attendance, study time, previous scores, tutoring, motivation and access to resources that can support a risk-scoring and recommendation layer after the primary regression task.
3. PROBLEM STATEMENT 
Existing student-performance systems demonstrate that Machine Learning can predict academic outcomes, but many approaches focus either on classification, specialized learning-platform activity, or prediction without actionable explanations. Our project aims to develop a multi-factor system using the Student Performance Factors dataset that predicts continuous examination scores, identifies students who may require attention, explains predictions through feature importance and SHAP, and converts the identified weaknesses into practical improvement recommendations. 
4. PROPOSED SOLUTION 
4.1 Final Marks Prediction
Develop a regression-based Machine Learning model to predict the student’s 
Exam_Score
 using academic, behavioral, and contextual factors available in the dataset.
4.2 Comparative Model Development
Train and compare multiple regression models:
Linear Regression
Decision Tree Regressor
Random Forest Regressor
Gradient Boosting Regressor
XGBoost Regressor
The models will be evaluated using MAE, MSE, RMSE, and R², with 5-Fold Cross Validation used for reliable model comparison.
4.3 At-Risk Student Detection
Use the predicted examination score to classify students into performance/risk categories using predefined score thresholds. This converts the regression output into an early-warning indicator for students who may require academic support.
4.4 Explainable AI
SHAP to explain individual predictions and identify how factors such as attendance, study hours, previous scores, tutoring, and other available features contribute to the predicted score.
Random Forest/XGBoost feature importance will provide global insights into the most influential factors.
4.5 Personalized Recommendations
Generate rule-based recommendations from the student's influential factors and predicted performance. For example, students showing low attendance or study hours can receive targeted suggestions for improvement.
4.6 Analytics Dashboard
Develop an interactive interface to display:
Predicted Exam Score
Risk Category
Key Influencing Factors
SHAP Explanation
Feature Importance
Performance Visualizations
Personalized Recommendations
5. OBJECTIVES
5.1 Exam Score Prediction
To develop regression models capable of predicting continuous student examination scores from academic, behavioral and contextual factors.
5.2 Comparative Model Evaluation
To compare multiple regression algorithms and identify the most suitable model using MAE, MSE, RMSE and R².
5.3 Risk Identification
To derive an interpretable risk category from predicted performance and identify students who may require additional academic support.
5.4 Explainable AI
To use SHAP and feature-importance analysis to explain both global model behavior and individual predictions.
5.5 Personalized Recommendations
To generate practical recommendations based on influential student factors identified through analysis and model explanations.
5.6 Analytics Platform
To present predictions, risk indicators, influential factors and recommendations through an interactive analytics interface.
